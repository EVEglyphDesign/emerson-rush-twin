Fourth mirror · A handbook, in the working lineage of Lilian

Lillian’s Guide

Transformational program management for information & technology.

Information and technology, held with provenance and portability, becomes a capital asset on the client’s balance sheet — and appreciates there. This is a handbook on how a global transformation programme is led so that the appreciation lands with the customer, and stays.

For this reader

This mirror of the handbook is prepared for a Group Platforms conversation inside a multi-brand enterprise that has already made SAP its foundation, is preparing the next wave of its S/4HANA journey through a business-led, clean-core, satellites-and-BTP posture, and is currently completing discovery deliverables in advance of solution design.

The vocabulary throughout is chosen to sit inside that posture. Nothing in the handbook asks the reader to translate; the practice is written in the terms already in use in the room. The document names no client and no engagement — it is offered here as something the reader can forward inside their own organisation without needing to redact it first.

What the handbook contains

Part 0 — EVE, and where it stands in a clean-core landscape

EVE — Enterprise Value Engineering — is the econometric utility ledger built as an extension of the substrate the enterprise already owns: SAP’s ACDOCA line-item table and its master-data companions, reaching outwards through SAP Datasphere. EVE sits as a satellite, inside the clean-core envelope, and runs on the extension platform the enterprise has already committed to. It does not modify the core; it contributes semantics upward to the group data platform through the same integration principles the enterprise’s own architects have written.

Part I — What is actually the case

Four observations that become obvious once seen. Information and technology has quietly become a capital asset. The return on that asset is realised elsewhere in the business, not inside the IT column — and in a multi-entity, multi-brand enterprise, that return also shortens the distance between what an operating brand knows about its own performance and what the group can see at consolidation. The asset appreciates when the customer holds it. The methods are portable and written down; the judgement to apply them lives with the person doing the work.

Part II — How the work is done

Habits, not rules, of a practitioner working in this lineage: naming the appreciating asset before the charter, marking the return where it lands, owner-led custody from the first drawing, keeping the satellite outside the core, keeping the mirror separate from the analysis, working from the cheapest source that answers, treating provenance as a delivery, interrupting the sponsor only at the boundary of expensive spend, making the effort visible, testing two exits, publishing the method and keeping the discretion.

Part III — What “Sovereign” means, precisely

A technical word for a specific arrangement checkable in an afternoon: custody of data, models, and keys under the customer’s control; authority to sign material changes with customer staff; a tested exit to two alternatives; and a return booked outside the IT function on the ledgers where it is actually realised. A programme running a business-led, clean-core doctrine already passes most of this checklist; the practice’s contribution is the discipline of saying so, in writing, at solution-design time, before the shape of the answer forecloses the option.

Part IV — The AI impact adaptations

Three named adaptations to what the picture already does, so a programme written today does not carry the design decisions of a programme written two years ago. OpEx efficiency is held inside the existing SAP estate. CapEx strategy is calculated inside the sovereign structure, on the customer’s own instruments. PMO and business-case quantification is based on the enterprise’s own source data — neither number estimated from a benchmark. The two-worlds structure and the ACDOCA spine do not change; the discovery scope defined today is written knowing what the tools of today can now do.

Part V — The Discovery AI tool

The assessment layer named as a thing so it can be built, measured, and handed over. Four functions, in order: analyse source sovereign data wherever it is today; ingest all existing third-party vendor materials into an AI-efficient repository; identify quantitative economic benefits, prioritising technical leakage first and holding future-state barriers alongside; identify early value-capture targets and begin feeding Decision Intelligence worklists to existing operational systems. Output is not a report; it is worklists into systems the enterprise’s own people already use. The tool is not a product — it stays inside the customer at the end of the engagement and continues to run.

Part VI — Inside the SAP AI landscape

A map of the current SAP AI construction — Joule as the umbrella experience, with Joule Assistants embedded in the applications, Joule Agents as non-deterministic workflow agents, Joule Studio as the build lane, Joule for Developers and SAP AI Core underneath, and SAP Business AI as the enterprise-wide brand. Beneath the AI surface: SAP Business Data Cloud as the current umbrella architecture, with SAP Datasphere named by SAP as its knowledge core, alongside SAP Knowledge Graph, SAP HANA Cloud, SAP Databricks, SAP Analytics Cloud, and the Data Marketplace. The renaming trail is named as a fact of life — deploy under today’s badges, expect one rename mid-programme, and attach roadmap milestones to underlying capabilities. The line drawn is complementary: where Joule stops, EVE begins — not by weakness but by scope. And SAP Datasphere before conversion is reframed as a discovery instrument, not a target-state fabric — pre-deployed against the current estate to catalog it, so discovery moves from memory to measurement.

Part VII — PAIX, and the wider work

The part of the practice that sits above any individual engagement. Going to where people are — helping the communities a practitioner lives in use the tools that now sit within reach of anyone with a browser, whether or not those communities will ever be customers. And staying with people after the engagement ends: when a career needs a hand at recovery, when a call comes in long after a project is closed, we take the call. This is the habit of adding: a method, a tool, a name to call, a competent hand held out at the right moment.

Author’s note — a calibration line

Every honest practitioner keeps a self-correction near the desk. The one preserved in this handbook is the shape of the discipline the paper is measured against: the outcome is what sovereign data means, made speakable inside the enterprise by the enterprise’s own people. The entry point is decoding technical leakage. The delivery is worklists into existing operational systems — not new dashboards, not new meetings, not new committees. Any future revision that drifts from that outcome, that entry point, or that delivery is a drift.

Technological grounding

EVE as a satellite on the SAP foundation

The handbook is written in the working vocabulary of EVE — Enterprise Value Engineering — the econometric utility ledger built as an extension of the ledger substrate most enterprises already own: SAP’s ACDOCA line-item table and its master-data companions, reaching outwards through SAP Datasphere and any downstream structure the customer chooses to extend into.

EVE is pro-SAP. SAP is the foundation. Where others argue SAP is no longer relevant in the age of generative AI and sovereign data, EVE holds the opposite position — more strongly than the marketing does. The reason the work is possible at all is that the customer already owns a full ledger substrate in ACDOCA and Datasphere; nothing else in the market gives an enterprise that starting point.

EVE sits outside the ERP core and inside the clean-core envelope. It does not compete with the group’s data platform — it contributes to it, adding the enterprise-value axis to the semantics the platform already carries. It runs on the extension platform the enterprise has already committed to. The unit-economics case for the satellite is measured against the programme’s own benefits map, not against the ERP core.

Customer success is SAP success. That is SAP’s own frame, and it is the right one. The practice helps SAP’s customers get more out of the capital they have already committed, so that the value lands on the customer’s ledgers where it belongs.
