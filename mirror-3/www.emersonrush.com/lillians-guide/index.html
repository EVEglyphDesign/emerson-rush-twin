Lillian’s Guide

Transformational program management for information & technology.

Something has changed in the way enterprises hold their information and technology function, and it changed more quickly than most of the industry has noticed. Data platforms, models, and the operating rituals around them — when they are built with provenance and portability — behave, on the ledger and in the boardroom, like the appreciating property they in fact are. This handbook describes how a practitioner working in that way tends to work.

The word Sovereign appears in the document as a technical term for a specific arrangement of custody, authority, exit, and return — nothing more heraldic than that. The customer owns the asset; the consultant is a steward.

EVE, and why Lillian sits at the centre

EVE stands for Enterprise Value Engineering. It is the econometric utility ledger — the extension surface built on top of the ledger structure most enterprises have already adopted, SAP's ACDOCA and its master-data companions — reaching outwards through Datasphere and any downstream structure the customer chooses to extend into.

EVE is pro-SAP. SAP is the foundation. Where others argue SAP is no longer relevant in the age of generative AI and sovereign data, we hold the opposite position — more strongly than the marketing does. The reason the work is possible at all is that the customer already owns a full ledger substrate in ACDOCA and Datasphere; nothing else in the market gives an enterprise that starting point.

Customer success is SAP success. That is SAP’s own frame, and it is the right one. The practice helps SAP’s customers get more out of the capital they have already committed, so that the value lands on the customer’s ledgers where it belongs.

EVE names the pattern; Lillian is the practitioner in whose lineage the practice is faithfully carried. This is why the handbook bears her name.

What the handbook contains

Part 0 — EVE, and why Lillian sits at the centre

Names the pattern. EVE — Enterprise Value Engineering — is the econometric utility ledger extending customer-owned SAP/Datasphere substrates. Lillian is the practitioner in whose lineage the practice is faithfully carried.

Part I — What is actually the case

Four observations that become obvious once seen. Information and technology has quietly become a capital asset. The return on that asset is realised elsewhere in the business, not inside the IT column. The asset appreciates when the customer holds it, and depreciates on someone else’s timetable when they don’t. The methods are portable and written down; the judgement to apply them lives with the person doing the work.

Part II — How the work is done

Habits, not rules, of a practitioner working in this lineage: naming the appreciating asset before the charter, marking the return where it lands, owner-led custody from the first drawing, keeping the mirror separate from the analysis, working from the cheapest source that answers, treating provenance as a delivery, interrupting the sponsor only at the boundary of expensive spend, making the effort visible, testing two exits, publishing the method and keeping the discretion.

Part III — What “Sovereign” means, precisely

A technical word for a specific arrangement checkable in an afternoon: custody of data, models, and keys under the customer’s control; authority to sign material changes with customer staff; a tested exit to two alternatives; and a return booked outside the IT function on the ledgers where it is actually realised. A programme satisfying all four is Sovereign in this sense. Fewer, and the arrangement is described honestly by its ordinary name.

Part IV — PAIX, and the wider work

Pro bono humanitatis.

PAIX is the French word for peace, and the name for the part of the practice that sits above any individual engagement. Two habits are worth describing.

Going to where people are. The tools that used to take a room full of confusion and turn it into a clear set of requirements lived, for a long time, inside expensive enterprise stacks. That is no longer quite true. Tools now within reach of anyone with a browser do a great deal of that work, which means a practitioner can help the people in the communities they live in use them well — whether or not those communities will ever be customers. When a larger commercial system does eventually arrive, the requirements have been thought through, the vocabulary has been agreed, and the people who will run it already know what they are running.

Staying with people after the engagement ends. Client relationships come and go on their own schedule; the people met inside them remain, in a quieter way, part of the practice. When a career needs a hand at recovery, we help. When a call comes in with something difficult, long after the original engagement is closed, we take the call.

Taken together, this is a habit of adding — a method, a tool now within reach, a name to call, a competent hand held out at the right moment. Where we can add, we add. Where we cannot, we say so, and we point to someone who can.
