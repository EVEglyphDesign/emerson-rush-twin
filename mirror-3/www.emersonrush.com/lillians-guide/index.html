What the handbook contains

Part I — What is actually the case

Four observations that become obvious once seen. Information and technology has quietly become a capital asset. The return on that asset is realised elsewhere in the business, not inside the IT column. The asset appreciates when the customer holds it, and depreciates on someone else’s timetable when they don’t. The methods are portable and written down; the judgement to apply them lives with the person doing the work.

Part II — How the work is done

Habits, not rules, of a practitioner working in this lineage: naming the appreciating asset before the charter, marking the return where it lands, owner-led custody from the first drawing, keeping the mirror separate from the analysis, working from the cheapest source that answers, treating provenance as a delivery, interrupting the sponsor only at the boundary of expensive spend, making the effort visible, testing two exits, publishing the method and keeping the discretion.

Part III — What “Sovereign” means, precisely

A technical word for a specific arrangement checkable in an afternoon: custody of data, models, and keys under the customer’s control; authority to sign material changes with customer staff; a tested exit to two alternatives; and a return booked outside the IT function on the ledgers where it is actually realised. A programme satisfying all four is Sovereign in this sense. Fewer, and the arrangement is described honestly by its ordinary name.

Part IV — PAIX, and the wider work

The part of the practice that sits above any individual engagement. Going to where people are — helping the communities a practitioner lives in use the tools that now sit within reach of anyone with a browser, whether or not those communities will ever be customers. And staying with people after the engagement ends: when a career needs a hand at recovery, when a call comes in long after a project is closed, we take the call. This is the habit of adding: a method, a tool, a name to call, a competent hand held out at the right moment.

Technological grounding

EVE — Enterprise Value Engineering, on the SAP foundation

The handbook is written in the working vocabulary of EVE — Enterprise Value Engineering — the econometric utility ledger built as an extension of the ledger substrate most enterprises already own: SAP’s ACDOCA line-item table and its master-data companions, reaching outwards through SAP Datasphere and any downstream structure the customer chooses to extend into.

EVE is pro-SAP. SAP is the foundation. Where others argue SAP is no longer relevant in the age of generative AI and sovereign data, EVE holds the opposite position — more strongly than the marketing does. The reason the work is possible at all is that the customer already owns a full ledger substrate in ACDOCA and Datasphere; nothing else in the market gives an enterprise that starting point.

Customer success is SAP success. That is SAP’s own frame, and it is the right one. The practice helps SAP’s customers get more out of the capital they have already committed, so that the value lands on the customer’s ledgers where it belongs.

Before the first conversation

A mutual, plain-language NDA

A short confidentiality and referral undertaking for a first working conversation. Two pages of clauses, one page of signatures, one page of plain-language note. It is intentionally boring.

What it does. Mutual confidentiality. Any advice given in consultation is the Client’s to keep — act on it, share it with your advisors, or hand it to another firm to execute. If we are not the right fit, we help you find someone who is at no charge, and that referral posture stands for as long as you might need it.

The practice is not the paper. The practice is standing behind the advice, whether or not an invoice ever follows.
