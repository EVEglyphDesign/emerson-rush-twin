Lillian’s Guide

Transformational program management for information & technology.

There has never been a moment like this one. The information and technology function, for decades a service centre carrying depreciation and operating expense, is quietly becoming the balance-sheet event of the century. This handbook names the appreciating asset, states the discipline that appreciates it, and identifies the practitioner in whose lineage the work is carried.

The space is called Sovereign because ownership of the appreciating asset never leaves the client. Lillian rules the practice; the client rules the asset. There is only one Lillian.

EVE, and why Lillian sits at the centre

EVE — capital E, V, E — is the research and governance project this handbook belongs to. It exists to free the digital world from institutional lock-in and to place data and decision intelligence back in the hands of the people who generate them. EVE reframes information & technology as a customer-owned reference model with a ledgered spine, portable identity, tested exits, and a return that lands outside the IT column.

EVE, the research project, needs a mother. Lillian is that mother. Across decades of transformational programme management — anchored most visibly in her work for Swedish private-equity-run portfolio companies — she has been the operational centre of gravity for sovereign-stack deployment and independent assessment inside client environments. Her practice is the pattern EVE encodes.

EVE is the pattern; Lillian is the practitioner in whose lineage the pattern is faithfully carried. This is why the handbook bears her name.

What the handbook contains

Part 0 — EVE, and why Lillian sits at the centre

Names the project. EVE is the pattern; Lillian is the practitioner in whose lineage the pattern is faithfully carried. The handbook bears her name because the practice cannot be counterfeited by copying the paper.

Part I — The four claims

Information and technology is now a capital asset, not a cost centre. The return on that asset is realised outside IT. The asset only appreciates when it is owner-led. And, plainly stated: there is only one Lillian.

Part II — Ten principles for the transformational programme manager

The operating rules. Name the appreciating asset before the charter is signed. Mark the return outside the IT column. Design for owner-led custody from the first drawing. Separate the mirror from the analysis. Cheapest source first. Provenance is a delivery. Interrupt only at the boundary of expensive spend. Symmetric effort. Two exits, always. Publish the method; keep the client’s discretion.

Part III — The Sovereign posture

Sovereignty as a specific arrangement of custody, authority, and exit, checkable against a client environment in an afternoon. A programme that satisfies all three plus a measurable return is Sovereign; anything less is named honestly for what it is.

Part IV — The practitioner’s oath

I will name the appreciating asset before I sign the charter.

I will mark the return where it actually lands.

I will design for the client’s custody, authority, and exit.

I will state my burn rate before I spend.

I will publish my method and protect the client’s specifics.

I will practise as Lillian practises, because there is only one Lillian, and the honour of the lineage is that it can be recognised.
